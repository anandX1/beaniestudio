What you're walking into

The essentials without the fiction: how a match runs, why the game is built the way it is, and what it runs on. If you only read one section before following the game, read this one.

How a round runs

You drop into the wreck with your crew. Pull heavy fuel scrap — the heavy pieces take two people. Every step is a volume decision: run to move fast, crouch to stay unheard. When the horn sounds at the extraction pad, the door stays open for sixty seconds. Full rounds run five to ten minutes.

Why there is no radar

Any tracking screen ends the horror before it starts: give the Hunter a map and nobody hides, give the crew one and nothing is scary. STATIC removes the minimap for everyone, so sound becomes the only information that matters. That single decision is why a quiet corridor feels dangerous.

What the Hunter hears

Sprinting boots. Scrap slamming the floor. A locker shut one second too late. Panicked breathing at close range. Staying alive means staying quiet, and staying quiet means being slower — so the crew is always trading speed for silence, and the Hunter is always listening for the moment that trade goes wrong.

Built for every screen

STATIC runs inside Roblox on mobile, PC, and console — all in the same matches, with full crossplay. It is tuned to perform on iPhone-8-class phones, because a horror game nobody can run is a horror game nobody plays. If Roblox runs on your device, STATIC runs on your device.
